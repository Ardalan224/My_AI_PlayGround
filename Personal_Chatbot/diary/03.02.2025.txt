It was late afternoon, and I managed to catch the bus just as it was pulling away from the stop. The seats were mostly full, but I found one by the window and sank into it, earbuds in, music low enough to hear the hum of conversation around me. The city outside passed in blurs of sunlight and exhaust fumes.

A little girl sitting across the aisle was showing her mother a crayon drawing, and I remember smiling at her excitement. When she noticed, she proudly turned it toward me — a crooked sun and a stick-figure family. For a second, the day’s stress melted away. By the time I got off at my stop, I felt oddly lighter, as if a child’s drawing had reminded me that not everything needed to be complicated.
