It started raining halfway through my walk to the post office, a slow drizzle that quickly turned into a downpour. I didn’t have an umbrella, of course, so I ducked under a tree and waited, watching the water ripple across the pavement. Everything smelled clean and new, the earthy scent of wet soil mixing with the faint perfume of someone’s blooming garden nearby.

After a few minutes, I gave up on staying dry and started walking again, shoes squelching with every step. A couple running past me splashed through a puddle, laughing. I remember thinking that there was something freeing about not caring — about just letting the rain happen. By the time I reached home, I was soaked but strangely content.
